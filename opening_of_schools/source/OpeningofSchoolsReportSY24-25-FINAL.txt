Executive Summary

Each year District 65 administration provides an Opening of Schools Report to the School Board and community that reflects the
enrollment data as well as related program participation and demographic information as of Sept. 30, 2024. The information
presented within this report provides a basis for planning during the current year and for developing projections for future years. For
the purpose of this report, students must be enrolled on Sept. 30 of the reporting year and enrolled in courses.

The 2024-25 number of students served is 6,241:

   ●​   3,260 students at elementary schools,
   ●​   2,500 students at middle/magnet schools
   ●​   73 students are outplaced (SEES)
   ●​   60 services only or evaluation (SEES)
   ●​   58 students at Park School
   ●​   20 students at the Rice Education Center
   ●​   236 students in the JEH Education Center programs for children ages three to five
   ●​   34 children are served in the JEH Education Center programs for children birth to three years old (Family Center)

The overall ethnic distribution of students served for 2024-25 is 39% White, 23% Black, 22% Hispanic, 10% Multi-Racial, 5% Asian.
The remaining 1% of students are American Indian or Alaska Native, Middle Eastern or North African, or Native Hawaiian / Other
Pacific Islander.

Forty percent of the District 65 students are designated as low income. This designation includes any student from a family receiving
public aid, living in an institution for neglected or delinquent children, supported in a foster home with public funds, or eligible to
receive free or reduced lunches.

The average class size for general education core classrooms (i.e., language arts, math, science and social studies) in:

   ●​ K-2 is approximately 17, which is 26% below the maximum class size goal of 23
   ●​ 3-5 is approximately 18, which is 28% below the maximum class size goal of 25
   ●​ 6-8 is approximately 22, which is 21% below the maximum class size goal of 28

Limited School Choice

Permissive transfers, magnet schools, and magnet programs offer families and students a choice and the opportunity to attend a
school outside their attendance area. There are two magnet schools (i.e., Dr Bessie Rhodes School of Global Studies and Dr Martin
Luther King Jr Literary & Fine Arts School) and two magnet programs - i.e., African-Centered Curriculum (ACC) and Two-Way
Immersion (TWI). Families may apply for their child to attend another school or program by completing an application. The
application is made available to families via the district website beginning in December. Information is distributed during kindergarten
information nights and kindergarten registration. Applications are accepted from all District 65 schools and each student is assigned a
random number that is used in the selection process.

Magnet Program Selections

Per Policy 7:32 (Magnet Schools/Programs), siblings of students enrolled in a magnet program and students residing in the
attendance area of a school that offers a magnet program will be given preference. Applications for the African-Centered Curriculum
(ACC) program at Oakton are accepted for kindergarten through fifth grade. Students are selected in the order of the applicant’s
assigned random number. Of the 77 students enrolled in the ACC program, 46 (60%) reside within the Oakton attendance boundary
and 31 (40%) transferred from their attendance area school to attend the magnet program.

Table 9, shows enrollment numbers by attendance area school for the African-Center Curriculum (ACC) program. There are 77
students enrolled in the ACC magnet program.

Retentions

For the 2024-25 school year, less than10 K-8 students enrolled at a District 65 school were retained in the same grade.

School-Age Child Care Enrollment

Table 10 shows the school-age child care enrollment as of Sept. 30, 2024. A total of 464 students are served in the district’s
School-Age Child Care program.

Transportation

As of Sept 30, 2024 there were 1,574 students assigned a bus route.

Health Services

Illinois School Code mandates 90% compliance with health exam and immunization requirements for students. As of Sept. 30, 2024
96.5% of District 65 students were in full compliance with the state immunization and exam requirements.
